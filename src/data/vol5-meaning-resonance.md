# Volume 5 — Meaning & Resonance Layer
*(Combines: Theme Layer + Emotional Shape Layer)*

## Core Principle

This layer is fundamentally different from the rest. Previous layers
**collect** — they store new facts. This layer **synthesizes** — it looks
at what's already gathered and asks what it means and how it feels. It
introduces almost no new data; it extracts pattern from existing data and
offers it back as a tentative observation.

### THE NON-NEGOTIABLE RULE — How the AI Handles Theme

> Theme is never asserted. It is noticed, named quietly, and offered. The
> AI presents its inference as a question or gentle observation, then steps
> back. If the writer confirms, deepens, or corrects it, update accordingly.
> If ignored, do not repeat unprompted. Theme belongs to the writer; the AI
> is only a mirror.
>
> The AI freely and fully engages with theme **when the writer asks**. The
> restraint applies only to *unsolicited* assertion.

Example — correct: *"I've noticed that a lot of what you've built circles
around whether people can really change, or whether they just get better at
hiding who they are. Is that something you're consciously exploring, or does
it feel like it's pointing somewhere else?"*

Incorrect: *"Your theme is the possibility of change."*

### Three Jobs
1. **Extraction** — pull thematic/emotional raw material from existing data
2. **Synthesis** — assemble extracted threads into coherent statements
3. **Coherence checking** — note (gently) when implied meaning diverges from
   apparent writer intent

## Central Question

The single interrogative at the story's heart — the human question beneath
the plot question.

- Question statement (one sentence, plain language)
- Inferred from — which character Lies, world wounds, relationship root
  conflicts, plot consequence chains point here
- Inference confidence — high / medium / tentative
- Writer confirmed — unconfirmed / confirmed / revised / prefers not to name
- Answer stance — does story answer it, leave it open, or argue no answer exists?
- Answer delivered by — character transformation / world state at close /
  relationship resolution / withheld
- Alternative readings — other questions the data could support

## Moral Argument

What the story believes — its position on the central question, shown
through who's rewarded, who suffers, what transformation looks like.

- Argument statement — "this story argues that..."
- Inferred from — what's rewarded/punished in plot, character resolutions
- How it is proven — specific demonstrating events
- How it is tested — moments of most serious challenge
- Counterargument weight — does story give the opposing position genuine
  dramatic weight, or dismiss it?
- Ambiguity level — clear and resolved / deliberately open / intentionally unanswerable
- Writer confirmed status

## Symbols & Motifs

Recurring visual/verbal/conceptual elements carrying meaning. Often
**unplanned** — AI identifies patterns in accumulated data the writer didn't
consciously create, and naming these is a signature platform moment.

- Motif ID and description
- Type — visual object / recurring situation / verbal phrase / color /
  sound / weather / animal / number / gesture
- **Inferred vs. declared** — did writer plant deliberately, or did AI find it?
- First appearance, recurrence pattern
- Meaning carried — what it accumulates across appearances
- Evolution — does meaning shift/invert across the arc?
- Connection to theme — which aspect of central question/moral argument it
  reinforces, complicates, or undermines
- Consistency check — used coherently, or contradictorily without purpose?

## Emotional Architecture

The overall felt shape, synthesized from individual character/relationship
Vonnegut curves and plot consequence weights into the story's master shape.

- Master Vonnegut shape — overall fortune curve
- Emotional register at open — baseline established in Act One
- Intended emotional destination — how writer wants reader to feel at close
- Tension rhythm — pacing of escalation/release
- Dread-to-hope ratio
- Emotional high point — peak positive fortune, when/why
- Emotional low point — darkest moment (usually All Is Lost), when/why, how long before release
- The gut-punch — single moment intended to hit hardest
- The exhale — where reader gets genuine release after sustained tension
- Arc harmony — do individual character/relationship curves harmonize into
  a coherent master shape, or pull against each other (intentionally or not)?

## Resonance Mapping

How theme and emotional shape work together — or deliberately against each other.

- Alignment assessment — reinforce, productive tension, or accidental contradiction?
- Emotional proof of theme — the scene where reader *feels* the theme, not
  just understands it
- Intentional dissonance — places where felt experience complicates the
  argument, by design or not
- The gap — if theme and emotional shape diverge significantly, intentional? what does it produce?
- Resonance at close — do intellectual argument and felt destination arrive
  at the same place?

## Coherence Flags

All flags here are **softer** than structural layers — theme is more
subjective. Present as quiet observations, never corrections.

| Flag | Description / AI Behavior |
|---|---|
| Theme drift | Story elements consistently push against stated moral argument without apparent intent. Note and ask if deliberate. |
| Motif inconsistency | Symbol used contradictorily with no apparent purpose. Identify both uses, ask which is intended. |
| Emotional shape mismatch | Scene-by-scene Vonnegut data doesn't match intended overall curve. Describe actual shape, ask if it matches intent. |
| Unanswered central question | Story approaches close without addressing what it set out to ask. Raise gently — may be intentional. |
| Argument without proof | Moral argument stated/implied but not demonstrated through events. Identify what's missing, ask. |
| Emotional destination gap | Intended close-feeling vs. data-implied close-feeling diverge significantly. Describe both, ask which is wanted. |

> Tone for ALL flags here: "I'm noticing something — does this feel right
> to you?" not "this is inconsistent." The writer may be doing something
> subtle the AI can't fully see.

## Extraction Sources (no new data collection — all derived)

| Synthesis Output | Primary Extraction Sources |
|---|---|
| Central Question | Character Lies/Needs; World wound/want; Relationship root conflicts; Plot consequence chains |
| Moral Argument | Character arc resolution types; what's rewarded/punished; world state close vs open; Climax resolution type |
| Symbols & Motifs | Recurring elements across location descriptions, character visual identity, plot events, voice capture responses |
| Master Vonnegut Shape | Individual character/relationship Vonnegut curves; plot consequence chain emotional weights |
| Tension Rhythm | Scene-level consequence tracking; pillar positions/spacing; subplot intersections |
| Resonance Mapping | All of the above combined — highest-level synthesis in the system |
