# Volume 6 — Visual, Sensory, Style & Prose
*(Combines: Visual/Sensory Layer + Style/Prose Layer)*

## Core Principle

Both halves are about **how the story is perceived** — one through senses,
one through language. Both are primarily **consistency/coherence tools**: a
reader shouldn't feel they've wandered into a different story because
prose register or sensory palette shifted without reason.

### Declared vs. Natural (critical distinction throughout this volume)

Both layers are built from two sources: what the writer **declares** they
want, and what patterns the AI **extracts** from voice capture responses and
accumulated material. Writers often think they write one way and naturally
do another. The AI holds both without privileging either, and surfaces the
gap as useful information — never a correction.

# PART ONE — VISUAL & SENSORY

## Sensory Palette

- Primary sense — sight/sound/smell/touch/taste (default = sight; departures should be intentional)
- Sensory hierarchy — ranked frequency of sense use across the story
- Sensory gaps — senses consistently ignored (deliberate or default)
- Detail density — granular close focus / impressionistic broad strokes /
  varies deliberately by scene type and intensity
- Subjective vs. objective — filtered through character perception/emotion, or neutral?
- Declared intention vs. extracted pattern vs. **gap** between them

## Visual Grammar

How the story sees:

- Camera distance — extreme close-up / mid-range / wide establishing — does it shift with intent?
- Movement style — static/contemplative, kinetic/action-driven, or
  deliberate shifts by scene type
- Light and shadow — descriptive and emotional tool usage
- Color vocabulary — frequent/avoided colors, consistent symbolic weight?
- Visual rhythm — how long descriptions run before moving on (linger vs cut)
- Focus selectivity — what's noticed vs ignored (the choice is itself expressive)

## Sensory Motifs

The felt equivalent of symbolic motifs (Vol 5). Often unplanned — AI
identifies patterns and offers them back as observations.

- Motif ID and name, sense engaged
- Inferred vs. declared
- First appearance, recurrence pattern and conditions
- Meaning carried, evolution across arc
- **Connection to symbol registry** — cross-ref Vol 5 if this is the sensory
  expression of an identified symbolic motif

> Example platform-signature moment: "I keep noticing that your character
> smells rain every time she's about to make a decision she'll regret — is
> that something you put there deliberately, or did it just happen?"

## World Atmosphere (cross-ref from Vol 4, fully specified here)

- Dominant sensory texture at story open
- Atmospheric shifts at each of the 5 pillar points
- Location-specific sensory profiles — sound/smell/feel beyond visual
- Weather/environment as emotional register — mirrors or contrasts
  emotional state at key moments
- Atmosphere as character pressure — how sensory texture oppresses,
  unsettles, or relieves the protagonist

# PART TWO — STYLE & PROSE

## Voice Profile

The most important atom in this volume — fed primarily by the Voice Capture
system (Vol 1).

- Declared intention vs. extracted pattern vs. **intention/instinct gap**
  (stored without judgment, as calibration data)
- Register — formal / literary / colloquial / vernacular / genre-inflected /
  hybrid; consistent or scene-dependent?
- Sentence energy — long/complex, short/percussive, or varied with a clear
  pattern of when each is used
- Prose density — spare/stripped, richly layered, or calibrated to scene type/intensity
- Emotional temperature — cool/observational, warm/immersed, or variable
- Trust level — how much prose trusts reader to infer (explain vs imply, show vs tell)

## Point of View & Narrative Distance

- POV mode — first / close third / distant third / second / omniscient / shifting
- Narrative distance — inside the body / slightly outside / removed
- Reliability — trustworthy / unreliable / self-deceived / spectrum — does it shift?
- Distance shifts — deliberate pull-back/move-closer at specific moments — what triggers?
- Tense — past / present / shifting (what governs?)
- Multiple POVs — how distinguished on page, transition handling, what each uniquely accesses

## Dialogue Register

Distinct from Voice Capture (which tracks behavior) — this tracks how
speech is **rendered in prose**.

- Dialogue density — how much of story is dialogue vs narration
- Dialect/idiolect — individual speech patterns rendered on page, how far committed
- Subtext rendering — how much of unsaid meaning is surfaced in prose vs left to inference
- Tag style — plain attribution / action beats / mix — consistent or variable?
- Silence and pause — how unfinished sentences, loaded pauses, subject changes are handled
- Voice distinction — recognizable per-character without tags?

## Rhythm & Pacing

- Scene pacing modes — how action/emotional/transitional scenes are
  rhythmically distinguished
- White space philosophy — paragraph breaks, section breaks, chapter
  endings as pacing tools
- The slow moment — how time-dilation at high intensity is rendered
- The fast cut — how low-intensity transitional material accelerates
- Chapter/section length philosophy — long immersive vs short punchy vs
  varied by phase
- Sentence length as rhythm — variation controlling pace/emphasis/impact

## Genre Register

- Genre baseline — default prose conventions for this genre
- Adherence level — embrace / subvert / deliberate tension / ignore
- Tonal range — darkest to lightest moment, controlled or not?
- Reader contract — what the opening pages promise, honored across the story?
- Reader trust — infer vs guide/explain

## Coherence Flags

| Flag | Description / AI Behavior |
|---|---|
| Register drift | Prose voice shifts between sections without apparent purpose. Identify sections, ask if intentional. |
| Distance inconsistency | Narrative pulls closer/further unexpectedly without motivation. Note and ask. |
| **Dialogue register mismatch** | Character speaks contrary to established voice profile. **Most common/jarring draft error — flag with HIGHER frequency than other flags.** |
| Sensory palette rupture | Scene's senses/density wildly inconsistent with established palette without reason. Describe inconsistency. |
| Pacing rupture | Scene rhythm jarring vs adjacent scenes without apparent purpose. Identify scene, ask. |
| Motif inconsistency | Sensory motif contradicts established meaning without purpose. Name both uses, ask which is intended. |
| Intention vs instinct gap | Declared style and natural patterns diverge significantly. Raise gently, once. |
| Reader contract breach | Story departs significantly from tonal/genre promise of opening pages. Note, ask if intentional. |
